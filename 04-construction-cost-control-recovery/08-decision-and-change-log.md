## Event: a supplier misses a milestone and gives an optimistic recovery date
**What happened (Week 18):** ClearSpan Glazing missed its window delivery milestone and said it would be "about 2 weeks late".

**How I challenged it, using evidence**
1. Asked for the **factory production schedule** and shipping booking. Glass for floors 1–3 was not yet scheduled for production, so a 2-week delay was impossible.
2. Built a realistic date from that evidence: **5 weeks late** (delivery Week 21 instead of Week 16).
3. Checked the **critical path**: the envelope must close before freeze-up (Week 24), or MEP and interiors stop.
4. Checked the **contract**: the late-delivery clause allows recovery of direct costs caused by the delay.

**Options assessed**
| Option | Schedule | Cost | Risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Accept the supplier’s 2-week claim and wait | Likely 5+ weeks; interiors stop in winter | Heating & standby later ~$400K+ | High | No |
| B. Temporary enclosure + heat for floors 1–3; MEP starts behind enclosure; windows installed as they arrive | Envelope Week 26 (+2 weeks); critical path protected | +$145K (from construction contingency; part recoverable from supplier) | Medium | **Yes** |
| C. Re-procure windows from another supplier | New lead time 14 weeks – worse | Cancellation costs + price premium | High | No |
